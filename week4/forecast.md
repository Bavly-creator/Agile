## How we got the numbers

1. **Commitment test.** Starting from the top of the backlog, we pulled stories into an imaginary one-week Sprint until the Developers said "this is all we can finish": S01, S02, S03 and S04 = 3 + 2 + 5 + 3 = **13 points**. This is the baseline velocity.
2. **Capacity cross-check.** 5 members × 2 project days per week = 10 person-days (see the capacity table below); 10 × 0.8 (20% buffer) = **8 person-days per Sprint**. 13 points is about 1.6 points per person-day, which fits the size of the stories, so no stories were removed.
3. **Forecast.** 4 Sprints × 13 points = 52 points can be delivered. The top 14 stories add up to 51 points, so **all four MVP epics fit** with 1 point of room. The Product Owner drew the MVP line after S14. The remaining 21 points (S15–S19) wait for a later release or become stretch stories in Sprint 4.

## Reflection on the estimation discussion

Estimating the backlog showed that the stories with hidden rules, such as the weekly group challenge (S14) and the time-zone handling (S16), are the hardest to size, because the Product Owner first has to decide exactly what is included. Splitting the first streak story into three smaller ones (S06, S09, S16) made the stories easier to estimate and moved the risky midnight logic out of the MVP. The velocity of 13 points per one-week Sprint is only a baseline guess, so after Sprint 1 we will replace it with our real velocity and move the MVP line if needed.
